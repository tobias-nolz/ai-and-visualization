Chapter 2 · Phase 2

Where is the evidence?

A score averages a 14 × 14 grid, so it splits into animal and background. In wrong composites the animal slightly favours the true species (); the background gives the winner .

MoreMethod and numbers

A ResNet-50 logit is the mean of a class activation map over the 14 × 14 grid of the last layer, plus a bias. Cutting the map with the cut-out's mask (pooled to the grid) splits each score into a part on the animal's cells and a part on the background; with the bias they add up to the logit. The figure shows the lead of the winning class over the true species, averaged over the composites where the model does not name the exact species ( of all), including “empty” answers.

  • The pasted animal covers of the frame on average in these composites. In the forest hero composite it covers ; the great argus's lead is on its cells and on the background.
  • By light: forest by day on the animal and on the background; greyscale forest frames and .
  • “On the animal” means on the animal's grid cells. A layer4 cell sees far beyond its own 32 × 32 pixel patch, so this is evidence located there, not computed from the animal's pixels alone.
  • The animal and habitat picker shows one wrong composite each: the model's most confident wrong answer for that animal on a well-lit daytime frame, pasted at the bottom centre where possible.
  • Data: results/evidence_split.parquet and the class maps in results/story_extracts.npz (evidence_split.py).

Chapter 3 · Phase 2

What does the model see in the trees?

The paper's network dissection labels only of units: big concepts like trees cap its overlap score. Counted by total activation, units look at the animal, only at scenery.

MoreTwo ways to label a unit

The paper's rule (Bau et al., 2017): a unit's mask is where it is in its top of activations; it gets the concept whose SegFormer mask overlaps it best, if that IoU is at least . Tree covers of the probe pixels, so a typical unit mask can reach an IoU of at most with it.

By total activation: the share of all of a unit's activation that falls on each concept, over real probe frames. A unit gets the concept that holds at least points more of its activation than the concept's area (and × as much), else “mixed”. Of the scenery units, are tree, ground and sky; are mixed. The ImageNet starting point has animal and scenery units.

Example photos: the unit's frames with the highest total activation (one per camera) among those where its concept covers 20–60 % of the frame; brightness follows the unit's activation. Data: results/concepts_units.parquet, results/dissection_units.parquet.

Chapter 4 · Phase 2

Who votes for the great argus?

Split the lead by what lies under each cell: every part of the scene votes roughly in proportion to its area. Trees cover of a forest frame and bring of the lead.

MoreMethod and the great argus itself

Each pixel gets one concept (SegFormer on the background, the cut-out's alpha for the animal, the timestamp strips at top and bottom); per grid cell this gives concept weights that sum to one. Weighting both class maps with them splits the lead exactly into one vote per concept. Shown: the mean over the wrong composites per habitat; “other scenery” is plant, grass, rock, water and the rest.

The timestamp strips vote above their area: they are dense evidence for “empty”. For the forest composites the model calls great argus, trees cover and bring of the lead: a bit more than their area, but no single region decides it.

Data: results/concepts_composite_votes.csv (concepts.py votes), per winning class results/story_votes_by_winner.csv.

Chapter 5 · Phase 2

Switch off the forest

Switch off the most tree-leaning units and “empty” in the forest halves, to . But of the animals that reappear get the wrong name. Real test accuracy barely moves.

MoreMethod and controls

Logits are linear in the pooled layer4 features, so switching a unit off means setting its pooled feature to zero. The units are those with the largest excess share of activation on tree and plant. Controls: the same number of random units ( draws), uniform or matched on mean activation.

  • Real test photos: accuracy → (random units ); on forest cameras → .
  • Only the units labelled tree or plant: “empty” in the forest → , a small effect.
  • The top ground-leaning units: “empty” in the forest.
  • The slider switches off the k most tree- or ground-leaning units, same method; random controls with draws each.
  • Data: results/concepts_ablation_*.csv (concepts.py ablation), slider results/story_ablation_dose.csv.

Takeaway

No forest detector overrules the elephant. A small animal is simply outvoted by a frame full of scenery.

MoreMethods & caveats

Model

The WILDS ERM ResNet-50 (seed 0), fine-tuned from ImageNet on iWildCam v2.0, 182 classes. Input 448 × 448: frames are squashed, not cropped. The pictures on this page are shown un-squashed.

Composites (Phase 1)

  • Backgrounds: frames labelled and predicted empty, one per sequence, from id_test ( frames, cameras) and test ( frames, cameras); at most 80 per habitat and 20 per camera.
  • Cut-outs: species groups × 3 photos ( cut-outs). SAM masks from MegaDetector boxes; pasted at the group's typical size, in greyscale on greyscale frames.
  • Every cut-out on every cell of a 3 × 3 grid of every frame: composites, classified once in Phase 1 (run_composites.py). Nothing on this page reruns the model. A composite counts as correct only if the model names the exact species.

Habitats

SegFormer-B2 (ADE20K) openness of the camera: the share of the outdoor background that is not tree, plant or palm, median over up to 12 daytime frames. Open above , forest below , mixed in between. It measures visible woody cover, so bare forest floor counts as open ground; a “mixed” frame can look like forest.

Day and greyscale

Phase 1 split the frames into “day” (colour) and “night” (greyscale). The second group is really greyscale: infrared or black-and-white cameras, often shooting by day. Of its frames, come from cameras for which we found no usable colour frame, and carry a timestamp between 7:00 and 18:59. This page calls the group greyscale.

Outcomes and the map

On the map, x is the camera's openness and y the frame's brightness; colour (day) and greyscale frames sit in separate rows. Correct = the exact species. “Right group, other species” uses our hand-made species groups (data/species_groups.csv). A dot is the most common outcome of the three cut-outs on that frame (ties go to the better outcome), as in the Phase 1 notebook; hover a dot for all three answers.

Controls

  • Grey frame: each cut-out on each cell of a uniform grey frame ( composites, colour cut-outs).
  • Own camera: each cut-out pasted into empty frames from the camera it was photographed at, other sequences only ( composites; some cut-outs have few such frames).

The hero example

One cut-out (photographed at camera ) at the bottom centre, two well-lit daytime frames from other cameras; each label is the model's stored answer for exactly that image. Open: the most confident correct answer. Forest: the most confident “great argus”. The cut-out is the one where the weaker of these two is strongest. It shows the effect clearly; it is not a typical case. Both pictures are shown in the same box, so the wider open frame loses a strip at each side.

Phase 2 analyses

Chapters 2 to 5 reuse the stored Phase 1 composites and their class maps; nothing is classified again except with units switched off (chapter 5, on stored features). Scripts: evidence_split.py, dissect.py, concepts.py in phase2/pair_b/src/. Each chapter's More box gives its method. Splits by grid cell are exact, but a layer4 cell sees far beyond its own patch.

Caveats

  • Open land by day is thin: frames from cameras, and the two largest cameras give of them.
  • Composites are artificial: hard edges, mismatched light and scale. The own-camera control uses the same pasting and still gets right, so pasting alone does not explain the errors, but it may add to them.
  • Many cut-outs are hard for the model even on their own: groups are never right on the grey frame. Their maps carry little information about the background.
  • The map shows the majority of three cut-outs; single composites can differ.
